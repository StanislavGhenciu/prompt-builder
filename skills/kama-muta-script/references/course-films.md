# The course films, beat by beat

These are the films shown in the course, broken down onto the One Camera graph. Quotes are verbatim from the course transcript, translated where noted. Picture descriptions come only from what the instructor says about them; the transcript has no visuals. Anything marked *[interpretation]* is this skill's reading, not the instructor's words.

Use these films to calibrate tone, dialogue density, where the dark night sits, and how little is said at the kama muta peak. The beat labels in the tables are this skill's mapping onto the One Camera graph, unless the course states the beat itself.

## DSM: *The Unsung Heroes of Science* (hero film, scripted; `00:09:45–00:14:20`)
**Brief.** Royal DSM, a B2B life-science and materials company. Several protagonist storylines (entrepreneur-scientists) are intercut. McKee cites it in *Storynomics* (p. 144). It has 1.4M organic views and won awards worldwide.

| Beat | Lines from the film |
|---|---|
| Want and resolve | "I can't abandon what I've started here. I need to keep going, you know?" |
| Internal conflict | "Maybe I should just get a normal 9:00 to 5:00 job." |
| Interpersonal conflict (home) | Child: "Always got something on, Dad. And why can't you spend time with us this weekend? Just a little bit. Just an hour." / "No promise. Daddy will be back soon. Okay? Do your best with Auntie Maria." |
| The mission (stakes) | Mosquito tubes against malaria; 9 billion mouths to feed; biodegradable plastics from methane; "5 million people around the world in wheelchairs who could be helped by this." |
| Interpersonal conflict (rejection) | "It's very inspiring, but obviously, this is not going to work for us." / "This has become an obsession, Dad. You don't even know if this is going to work." / "People like you are the reason our planet is gonna hell." |
| External conflict | "The flood situation in Thailand has gone from bad to worse." / "Our site's under 2 meters of water right now. It, it's all gone." / "They keep on asking us for more data. This has been going on for more than 20 years." |
| Turn | "You can't let one setback make you throw away your entire dream." |
| Stakes made human | "I dived off a surfboard without my hands out, and I broke my neck. So, all of the independence that I had is completely gone." / "So this is not about research. This is about lives of real people." |
| Kama muta | The disabled woman stands up in bionic legs and hugs her wife (`01:54:49–01:57:30`). "It just felt like I got to reclaim a bit of me that's been lost." |
| Peak line | "It's really gonna happen, Dad. I, yeah, I think we finally, we finally made it. I think this is gonna work." |
| Score | Song "I get up … I get up again." |

*[interpretation]* Every line is concrete and situational; nobody says "perseverance", yet the whole film is about it. The refrain "I get up again" carries the value instead of a character stating it. Struggle takes up most of the runtime, and the release is short.

## Canon: *A Maternidade* (human interest, documentary; `00:29:02–00:35:06`)
**Brief.** It scored the highest kama muta in the University of Amsterdam study, on a budget of about 15K including travel. It was found with the research question: "When have you experienced a moment in your career where you felt extreme insecurity?"

| Beat | Doctor's own words (VO / interview) | ≈ position (computed from course timecodes) |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis / hook | "I'm a doctor to save lives. Unfortunately, we cannot do miracles. But sometimes, really sometimes, miracles happen." | 0–4% |
| Inciting incident | "One day a patient came in … a cardiac twin … eventually they will die." Needs a laser procedure in Great Britain. | 5–18% |
| Obstacle | "Look, I cannot travel because I don't have documents to travel." "A nightmare." | 18% |
| Save the cat / value | "I'm a doctor, and I don't care about passports. My concern is to save lives." | 20% |
| Decision 1 | Phones around; a machine exists "in the military hospital … for more than 7 years and no one ever, ever used it." He decides to move the whole team there. | 22–35% |
| External / interpersonal conflict | Military hospital under the defence ministry, his hospital under the health ministry: "a nightmare in Portugal because all of these bureaucracies." Phone calls to "all people that I remember." | 35–50% |
| Sender's role (message seed) | "We are used to work with Canon … 'Just tell me when, the day and at what time, and the machine will be there.' In fact, the machine was there one day before the procedure." | 52–58% |
| **Dark night** | "I was extremely nervous … I have to do the 1st procedure in Portugal without the supervision of my mentor. What happens if it goes wrong? It can be the difference between life and death … If this is not going to work well, I can compromise all the plans that we had for a fetal surgery center in Portugal." | ≈66% |
| Act 3 | "It was a success … the procedure was less than 15 minutes." | 75% |
| Kama muta | She delivered in their hospital; "she's a very well-known … singer, and we did her delivery on her own music songs." The team comes together with the mother; the doctor is reunited with his patient (`01:54:49`, `01:59:00`). | 82–95% |
| Payoff | "Her daughter is now almost 11 years. That make us feel proud …" | last ~10% |

*[interpretation]* The whiff of death is double: the babies' lives and the doctor's dream of a fetal surgery centre. The sender (Canon) appears inside the story as one helping hand at about 55%. It is not the hero and is never mentioned again, which is why the message fits without storywashing. The idiosyncratic details (the unused 7-year-old machine, the delivery to her own songs) are what make it feel true.

## P&G: *Thank You, Mom* (hero film; 2012 Olympics; `00:52:14–00:55:54`)
- **Story.** The lifelong dedication of Olympic athletes' mothers: "waking up at the crack of dawn, driving their kids to practice, doing endless loads of laundry, comforting them after a fall." It is a coming-of-age story across multiple storylines, in about 2 minutes.
- **Dialogue.** Almost none: "Time to get up, baby." / "Come on." / "Up you get." Then music and crowd.
- **Save the cat.** The moms' quiet, tireless devotion.
- **Kama muta.** "A series of mini reunions between mother and child after every event"; tears welling in the mothers' eyes after the wins.
- **Message chain.** A brag ("we sponsor the Olympics and sell products that help moms…") becomes a human message ("We see you … we're proud to help in our own small way"), then the insight "The hardest job in the world is the best job. Thank you, Mom.", then the payoff "P&G, proud sponsor of moms."
- *[interpretation]* With no words, the struggle is carried by repetition of small, physical, early-morning acts. The repetition is the hardship.

## Dutch grower film (plotted by the course onto the graph; `01:24:56–01:29:31`; Dutch, translated here)
| Beat | Line (translated from Dutch) |
|---|---|
| Value planted (save the cat + theme) | Grandfather to grandson, walking the fields: "You know, when I was as old as you, I walked here just like this with grandpa. Above all, he taught me to persevere. Not to give up." |
| External conflict | Radio: "34 days without rain, and today we break the record for the number of days without precipitation." |
| Economic conflict / debate | "But who guarantees me that the cost price won't rise much further? Everything is getting more expensive. Then, as a grower, you start wondering whether this is still the road to take." |
| Interpersonal / decision pressure | "Yes, no, I get that. If it storms, we can't harvest. But if we wait now and it suddenly freezes, I lose everything." |
| Encouragement (connection) | "You're going to make dad proud." |
| Dark night | "It doesn't always go the way you want, boy. That's part of it too." |
| Turn | "Hey, keep your courage up, eh?" … [laughter] |

*[interpretation]* The value ("persevere, don't give up") is stated once at the start by a grandfather to a child, as inheritance rather than as a moral. The end pays it off without repeating it. That is a legitimate way to name the value early without being on the nose: it is spoken inside a relationship, about the past.

## Texas farmer: US Farmers & Ranchers Alliance (hero film, 2019; `02:11:28–02:19:22`)
**Context.** Jasper's comeback film after five years of not making story films. He reconciled with director Hugo to write it, and "can you see how much of what I'd been feeling was injected into this film?"

| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| VO-ADDRESSED opening | A father to his son: "To be honest, I don't know where to start. I was 6 when I knew what I wanted to be. You're only 2 now, but sometimes I swear I can already see it in your eyes." |
| Inheritance / want | "It was your granddad who got me into it, put me in charge of my own acreage at 16. Boy, I had so many ideas … I felt like I was on a mission." |
| Decision 1 consequence | "And if I'm really honest, I still can't fully wrap my head around how it didn't work." |
| Dark night (stated as a decision) | "Your mother and I are selling the farm, every single acre … the realtor is going up tomorrow morning … we're just gonna move to the city." |
| Conflict, 3 levels | Drought "getting worse every year … costs keep running up and prices keep going down." "One bad season can knock you and everyone you care about right over." "It seems like nobody cares … they just blame everything on you. And you, you start losing your pride, and that's no life." |
| Value / need | "The farm is everything to me … But my family means even more. So everything that I do, in the end, it's gotta be for you." |
| Turn (outside voices) | A debate clip: "are we a part of the problem or part of the solution?" "I think that we are the solution to climate change." Soil carbon, peers monitoring soils by GPS: "They're superheroes." "We only have about 30 harvest cycles left to get it right." |
| Peak line | "Let's go home." / "Yeah." |
| Message | "It's a very exciting time to be in farming because we will be one of the 1st industries to have a negative carbon footprint." |

*[interpretation]* Addressing the VO to a two-year-old makes every line intimate and lets the viewer overhear. The dark night is a concrete decision with a date ("tomorrow morning"). The peak is two words. The transcript doesn't describe the picture at the turn; that "Let's go home" means he keeps the farm is an inference from the dialogue.

## Jasper's KLM story: the kama muta lesson (`01:50:07–01:54:48`)
| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| Setup / want | Summer 2021, COVID: Europeans barred from the US. He quarantined two weeks to direct a shoot in North Carolina, alone with an American crew he'd never met. |
| Save the cat | "I missed my wife and my daughter … I'd never been away from my 7-year-old daughter this long, and she had been heartbroken when I left." |
| Inciting incident | After a week of 16-hour days: "my flight got canceled due to extreme weather at my layover in New York." |
| Decision 1 | Instead of a motel, on a rainy highway with an almost-empty phone, he books the last seat to Dallas to catch an early KLM flight. |
| External conflict | Rain, 45 minutes to the airport with an hour to the flight, a dying battery. |
| Dark night | A six-hour layover, "all alone in Terminal D … in a deserted airport." At 3 a.m. "I felt so incredibly lonely … so homesick." He panics that there is no flight or he's in the wrong terminal; "no one to ask, no one to call, and my battery had already died." |
| **Kama muta (reunion)** | "I looked up and there I saw it … the Dutch KLM cabin crew came marching in, bop, bop, bop, arms swinging, confident smiles, determined and heroic … Rushes of goosebumps washed over me, and tears of happiness rolled down my cheeks." At the gate the stewardess looks at his passport: "Nederlands?" "Ja." "I'm going home." |
| Lesson | "A reunion scene … took all the struggle … and converted it to raw emotions." |

*[interpretation]* All five ingredients are present:
1. Empathy: the missed daughter.
2. Suddenness: "I looked up and there I saw it".
3. Synchrony and connection: the crew marching in step.
4. Visible emotion: goosebumps and tears.
5. Arc: reunion with home and countrymen.

The words at the peak are a two-word exchange and three words.

## Negative models

**Renault Clio "30 years in the making"** (`00:22:37–00:25:25`, `00:56:04`). Two exchange students, a British girl and a French girl, fall in love. Separation, decades of letters and near misses, then her lover shows up at her doorstep as adults (`01:54:49`). The story and the kama muta are excellent. The payoff (the car has existed as long as their relationship) is storywashing. The fix direction: express the story's human value ("Bringing people together…").

**Vodafone *Kiss*** (`00:56:39–00:57:40`). A couple kissing through every life stage, a story of enduring love and the growing pains of life. "Good things should last forever" fits. "Vodafone limitless talk, a good thing" is the wrong scale: it equates lifelong companionship with a phone-plan feature.

**AkzoNobel *Look around you*** (`00:44:49–00:45:37`). The opening VO: "Look around you. What would life be like if wood was left to rot, metal rusted, and the walls cracked? The world would become colorless, lifeless, and devoid of inspiration … That's why AkzoNobel is focused on life's essentials…" It shows the identity-film signature: a voice that is nobody, no protagonist, messaging from the first second. It came from the same brief and budget as DSM.

**Horticulture "prodigal daughter"** (`01:04:10–01:05:36`). The premise was sound: a farmer's daughter becomes a city consultant and feels the pull home. But every motivation, line and scene was invented without research: "a perfect blueprint for a story filled with nothing but cliches." This is the exact failure this skill guards against when it writes from a thin source.
